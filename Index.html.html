Which health facility gets power first? (three tiers, highest priority first)

Inside the health sector, not every building carries the same risk when power drops. Pick a tier to load it into the planner and see what happens in a shortage.

Priority 1 · served first

Super-speciality hospital

Examples: heart, brain and transplant units, ICU and NICU, ventilators, operation theatres, dialysis, MRI and CT, blood bank.

Essential minimum: 88% of demand (200 of 226 kW)

Harm value: $10 per unserved kWh

Why this rank: patients depend on machines that keep them alive, harm can start within minutes, and these patients cannot be moved quickly.

Priority 2 · served second

Multi-speciality hospital

Examples: emergency room, general wards, labs, X-ray, common surgeries, smaller ICU, pharmacy cold storage.

Essential minimum: 60% of demand (90 of 150 kW)

Harm value: $6 per unserved kWh

Why this rank: it also treats emergencies, but with fewer life-support machines, and the most critical cases can be transferred to a super-speciality hospital.

Priority 3 · same tier as homes

Normal clinic

Examples: outpatient consultation, lights and fans, basic tests, computers, vaccine fridge.

Essential minimum: 28% of demand (25 of 90 kW)

Harm value: $3 per unserved kWh

Why this rank: visits can be rescheduled and patients can go to a nearby hospital. The vaccine fridge still needs steady power, so a small essential minimum keeps it on.

Reasons for choosing this order

  1. Time to harm. Loss of life support hurts within minutes. A vaccine fridge can last hours. A routine check-up can wait days.
  2. Can the patient go elsewhere? Clinic patients can. ICU patients cannot be moved quickly, so the facility that treats them is protected first.
  3. Share of life-support equipment. The more of a facility's load is life support, the larger its essential minimum.
  4. Medical risk, not size. The rank follows risk to patients, not the size of the building or its electricity bill.

These are example settings for the demo. A real deployment would use values agreed with hospital engineers and the utility. Every number can be edited in the planner on the System tab.
